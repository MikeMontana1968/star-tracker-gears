What changed in Rev B

fixed in the CAD

Drawing Rev A for this guide turned up eight conflicts. All are fixed in star_tracker_gears.scad, rebuilt, and checked for interference in OpenSCAD. Two need your hands: countersink the plate, which is already cut, and cut the shaft to 55 mm. If you printed Rev A parts, reprint the tower halves, both hubs and the bracket.

  1. KI 1

    Tower cone sat inside the plate. Rev A's 45° cone was 32 to 44 mm across inside a 22.4 mm hole, so the flange could never seat. Rev B: the halves clamp the plate. The flange is on top, a 41 mm disc is underneath, dowels cross the gap, and 3 × M3×14 thread-form into the disc. Step 13.

  2. KI 2

    Shaft ran through the camera nut. Rev B: cut it to 55 mm, z −33 to 22. Step 14.

  3. KI 3

    M3×10 motor screws bottomed out. Rev B: M3×8 flat head, 3.7 mm of thread. Step 5.

  4. KI 4

    Screw heads under the S1 wheel. The wheel is 2.0 mm above the plate, over two motor screws and one M5 wedge hole. Rev B: countersink those holes by hand. Step 5, step 19.

  5. KI 5

    Lower hub rubbed the tower. Rev B: a printed 7.2 × 1.0 washer sits on the bearing's inner race, and the hub is 1 mm shorter, so the wheel stays at 21.5 mm. Step 15.

  6. KI 6

    Bracket had no axial stop. Rev B: it slides up until it stops on the clamp disc, so the 2.0 mm magnet gap comes from the parts. Step 18.

  7. KI 7

    Grub-screw holes in gears that turn on fixed nails. Rev B: removed from both stage gear files. On Rev A prints, leave them empty. Step 6.

  8. KI 8

Which board?

Build board Rev C. It is laid out for the module in hand: the 30-pin ideaspark ESP32 + 0.96" OLED, rows 27.94 mm apart. Rev B was drawn for a 38-pin DevKitC and does not fit it.

The pin map was settled by the pin check (step 55): the OLED shares the sensor I²C bus on GPIO 21/22, so WAKE stays on GPIO 4, and the module has no PSRAM. Using a different ESP32 board? Run the pin check on it first.

This section is written for a first board. Parts go in one kind at a time, simplest first. Each kind gets two pages: how the part goes in, then a map of the board with every place it goes, its value, and a box to tick.

22

Soldering, the basics

a shiny cone that hugs the lead
a ball: too much, may not wet the pad
dull, grainy, a gap at the pad: reheat it
joins two pads: drag it apart or wick it off
  • Iron at 330 to 350 °C. Wipe the tip on damp sponge or brass wool, then melt a little solder onto it (tinning): a shiny tip moves heat.
  • Use 0.8 mm rosin-core solder. 63/37 leaded flows most easily; wash your hands after. Work with a fan or open window.
  • Parts go in from the printed side. Solder from the back. Trim each lead just above the cone with flush cutters, after it is soldered.

Never soldered before? Practise ten joints on a scrap of perfboard and some resistor offcuts first. It takes about fifteen minutes and it is the best quarter hour of the build.

Resistors: how

  • These resistors stand up. Bend one lead tightly over the top of the body and down beside it. The body stands over the circle; the bent lead goes into the other hole.
  • No polarity: either end can go up.
  • Measure every resistor with the meter on Ω before it goes in. It is quicker and surer than reading colours, and the colours below confirm it.

R14 and R15 are the 1 % pair that scales the battery voltage for the ESP32. Keep them apart from the ordinary 100 kΩ resistors: a swap makes every battery reading wrong.

Small transistors: how and where

  • The flat face of the body sits over the flat edge of the outline. The legs go straight down, middle leg into the middle hole.
  • Read the print on each one before it goes in. The 2N3904 and the 2N3906 look identical. Q3 and Q5 are 2N3904; Q4 is the 2N3906.
  • Leave 3 to 5 mm of leg above the board. The heat stays in the leg, not the transistor.

A swapped 2N3904/2N3906 does not smoke. The GPS simply never powers up, and it takes a long time to find. Read the print with a magnifier or a phone camera.

Power parts: how

  • Q1, Q2 IRF4905: the metal tab faces the bold bar on the outline, and pin 1 goes in the square pad. Printed face toward you, the legs read G D S.
  • U2 LM2596T-5.0: five legs in two rows, 1, 3, 5 in one and 2, 4 in the other. Pin 1 is the square pad. Clip the heatsink on before soldering, so it sits where it will live.

Quick check: first light

  • No modules in the sockets. Meter on Ω across J1: the reading climbs as C1 charges. A steady reading under 10 Ω is a short: find it first.
  • 12 V into J1 through a 100 mA current limit, or a 12 V bulb in series with the lead. + to the left screw (J1 pin 1, the square pad).
  • LED1 lights green. The meter reads 4.9 to 5.1 V at the ESP32 socket's VIN hole. LED2 and LED3 stay dark. Nothing gets warm.
  • Power off. All good? On to the full tests in section C. Not good? Section C's tests, one at a time, show which part is at fault.

This is the first time the board does something. If LED1 lights, the input protection, the fuse and the 5 V regulator are all in and working.

C

Smoke test

Nothing expensive goes in until every rail reads right. A bench supply with a current limit, or a 12 V bulb in series with the lead, turns a mistake into a reading instead of a smell.

41

Test 0: DON'T HOOK UP POWER YET – Look, then measure resistance

How this works with no power. On Ω the meter sends a small current of its own and reads the voltage it makes. Across J1 that current fills the board's capacitors through the fuse and Q1, so the reading starts low and rises to megohms, then OL. A short never fills and stays low. The test: does it climb, or stay put?

Here the leads matter. Unpowered, Q1 passes the meter's current only from J1 + inwards. Red is + on most meters, so red on +12V gives the climb. Swapped, the meter jumps straight to OL: harmless, but no check.

  • 1. Red lead on J1 +12V, black on J1 GND. The reading starts low and climbs to megohms or OL (see the note). A reading that stays low is a short: find it before any power goes in.
  • 2. 5 V to GND and 3. 3V3 to GND, at the socket holes: they climb the same way, or settle at a few kΩ through the resistors on those rails. Neither may stay below 10 Ω.

Fit the driver and the clock

fitting, then a check

Not a measurement: this is where the last two modules go into their sockets. Each one fits two ways round; the blue rings show where its pin 1 goes.

  1. Power off and unplug the supply.
  2. Wire the motor first: do step 51, then come back. The driver must never be powered without its motor.
  3. Turn the TMC2209's VREF pot to its low end (fully anticlockwise) for now.
  4. TMC2209 into J30/J31: the module's EN pin into the hole marked EN. On BTT modules the chip is on the underside; that is normal.
  5. DIAG: no module brings it out on its header. Solder a short flying lead from the module's DIAG pad to J5 pin 1.

G2 · Controller board, logical diagram

  • Three rails. 12 V from the battery through F1 and Q1; 5 V from the LM2596 buck; 3.3 V from the ESP32 module's own regulator, about 20 mA of extra load.
  • Two loads are switched and off by default: the camera's 5 V (CAM_EN) and the GPS's 5 V (GPS_EN). Pull-downs hold both off while the ESP32 boots.
  • One wake line takes both the RTC alarm and the CONFIG button, wired-OR on a single 4.7k pull-up. Firmware tells them apart from the RTC's alarm flag.
  • Signal names, not GPIO numbers: FIRMWARE.md §4 maps each to its GPIO for board Rev C.

GPS UART, fixed. Rev B named the nets GPS_TX / GPS_RX and wired the ESP32's transmit to the module's TXD. design.py now names them by direction, ESP_TX_GPS_RX and GPS_TX_ESP_RX, and sends the ESP32's TX (GPIO 5) to J9 pin 3, the module's RXD. Board Rev C carries the fix.
